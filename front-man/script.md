# THE FRONT MAN: The Tahawwur Rana Story
### Long-form documentary script — YouTube (investigative character study)

**Runtime target:** 12–14 minutes
**Tone:** Investigative character study — slower, more psychological. Restructured from the original draft (`script_draft_v0.md`) to `../SCRIPT_RULES.md`; facts from `research.md` (current to October 2025 reporting).

## COLD OPEN (0:00–1:10)

**[VISUAL: Map of India, zooming to Mumbai. A single pin on the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
November 2008. A doctor from Chicago checks into the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel in Mumbai. He is there on business — his immigration consultancy has an office in the city. [PAUSE] About eleven days later, gunmen will be walking the same corridors.

He never fires a shot. He never plans a target. What he supplies is rarer: a cover story.

Three years later, an American jury clears him of helping plan the Mumbai attacks. [PAUSE] Cleared. And fourteen years after that, India flies him to Delhi to stand trial for them anyway. How can a man be acquitted of an attack — and still be sent home to answer for it?

**[TITLE CARD: THE FRONT MAN]**

## SEGMENT 1 — THE FRIENDSHIP (1:10–3:00)

**[VISUAL: Two silhouettes side by side, a line connecting them, labelled "Hasan Abdal · late 1970s".]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Everything here comes from the record — court filings, the NIA's case, and PBS Frontline's reporting. And every one of those accounts starts in the same place. [PAUSE] A military prep school in Punjab called Cadet College Hasan Abdal.

Tahawwur Rana was the son of a school principal. The other boy was born Daood Gilani. The world would later know him as David Coleman Headley. He had an American mother and a Pakistani father. Classmates never forgot his eyes — two different colours.

The two boys became close. Then their lives split apart. Rana went into medicine. Headley drifted into drug trafficking, then a stint as an informant for the US Drug Enforcement Administration, and eventually into radicalisation. [PAUSE] The friendship survived all of it. Rana's wife, Samraz, later told Frontline that the family considered Headley one of their own. He called her "sister." Her children called him "special uncle." [PAUSE] That trust is the whole story. Everything that follows runs through it.

---

## SEGMENT 2 — THE RESPECTABLE MAN (3:00–4:45)

**[VISUAL: Map line traces Lahore → Toronto → Chicago.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
On paper, Rana's life looked nothing like a terrorist's. He came from a respectable family: one brother became a psychiatrist in the Pakistan military, another a journalist in Canada. He trained as a doctor. He served as a Captain in the Pakistan Army Medical Corps. Then, in the late 1990s, he deserted. [PAUSE] The reasons are still unclear.

In 1997 he emigrated to Canada with his wife, also a physician. Records show both became Canadian citizens in 2001. From Toronto, he moved into business and settled in Chicago. There was a halal meat business on Devon Avenue. And there was First World Immigration Services — offices across North America and, eventually, a branch in Mumbai.

It was a real business, with real clients. [PAUSE] That is exactly what made it useful.

---

## SEGMENT 3 — THE COVER (4:45–7:00)

**[VISUAL: Map pin on Mumbai, then zooms to Taj Mahal Palace Hotel coordinate.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
According to Indian investigators, Headley used the Mumbai branch as his cover story. He travelled to India again and again as an immigration consultant. His real assignment, investigators say, came from Lashkar-e-Taiba's Hafiz Saeed and Zaki-ur-Rehman Lakhvi: surveil the targets.

Rana made his own trip roughly eleven days before the attack. He stayed at the Taj — one of the buildings that would soon be under siege. NIA records place him on other trips under the same consultant cover. [PAUSE] Agra. Kochi. Hapur.

**[CITATION CARD: Sources — NIA case records via Free Press Journal; Deccan Herald timeline]**

So was he an unwitting friend? Or an informed participant? [PAUSE] In early 2009, months after the attack, Headley phoned Rana in Chicago. He wanted to play him a recording — senior Lashkar commanders directing the ten gunmen in real time. One voice impressed Rana. It belonged to Sajid Mir, an alleged mastermind who remains at large. And Maharashtra's Anti-Terrorism Squad says it holds recordings of both men congratulating Lashkar's leadership on the "excellent planning and execution."

---

## SEGMENT 4 — THE PLOT THAT CAUGHT THEM (7:00–8:15)

**[VISUAL: Map line, Chicago → Copenhagen.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Mumbai wasn't the only operation running through this friendship. The two men were also drawn into a plot against the Danish newspaper Jyllands-Posten, which had published cartoons of the Prophet Muhammad in 2005. Court documents describe a plan to attack the paper's staff.

It was never carried out. [PAUSE] Instead, it caught them. On October 18, 2009, federal agents arrested both men at O'Hare Airport. [PAUSE] It was almost a year to the day after Mumbai.

---

## SEGMENT 5 — THE SPLIT (8:15–10:00)

**[VISUAL: A courtroom-adjacent graphic — two columns, "Acquitted" and "Convicted," filling in.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Headley pleaded guilty in March 2010. His deal brought 35 years in prison — and protection from extradition to India. [PAUSE] Then he took the stand against his oldest friend.

Rana's trial opened in a Chicago federal court on May 16, 2011. It ran until June 9. His defence said he had been manipulated. He believed, his lawyers argued, that Headley's assignments came from Pakistan's intelligence service, the ISI — not a terrorist group. Prosecutors answered with emails, wiretaps and Rana's own words after his arrest.

The jury split the verdict in two. [PAUSE] Not guilty of supporting the Mumbai attacks — the event at the heart of this story. Guilty of supporting the Denmark plot, and of material support to Lashkar-e-Taiba. Judge Harry Leinenweber called it "a dastardly plot" and sentenced him to fourteen years, plus five years of supervised release.

**[LOWER THIRD: "2011 verdict — Acquitted: Mumbai conspiracy · Convicted: Denmark plot + LeT material support · 14 years"]**

Not convinced enough for Mumbai. Convinced enough for everything around it. [PAUSE] And that gap would decide the next fourteen years.

---

## SEGMENT 6 — THE LOOPHOLE (10:00–11:45)

**[VISUAL: A simple legal-diagram motif — a US flag icon and an India flag icon, with a gap between them labeled "different charges."]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Here is the hinge. In the US, Rana was tried for one specific crime connected to Mumbai — and acquitted. But India's charges were different. Murder. Waging war against the state. Terrorism under Indian law. [PAUSE] Same events. Different crimes.

India had issued a warrant in 2018. Then came an odd twist. In 2020, Rana was released early from a US federal prison on COVID grounds. [PAUSE] He was free for days. He was re-arrested — this time on India's extradition request, filed formally that June. Rana fought it for nearly five years. A magistrate certified the extradition. An appeals court upheld it. He took it all the way to the Supreme Court. His last argument was that he would face torture in India, in breach of the UN Convention Against Torture. [PAUSE] It didn't work.

The Ninth Circuit had already answered the core question. The crimes charged in India, the court wrote, have "elements independent" from those he had faced in America. [PAUSE] Justice Elena Kagan turned down his final emergency application in March 2025. In April, the Court dismissed his last petition. [PAUSE] No appeals were left. Not one.

---

## SEGMENT 7 — THE HANDOVER (11:45–12:45)

**[VISUAL: Map line, Los Angeles → Delhi.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
On April 9, 2025, US Marshals handed Rana to India's National Investigation Agency. A special flight carried him from Los Angeles. [PAUSE] The next evening he landed in Delhi — and was arrested the moment he stepped off the plane.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Rana would face justice for his role in the Mumbai attacks. For India, it closed a chase that began with the 2011 verdict. [PAUSE] The trial it had wanted all along could finally begin.

**[LOWER THIRD: "April 10, 2025 — Rana lands in Delhi, formally arrested on arrival"]**

---

## SEGMENT 8 — UNFINISHED (12:45–13:45)

**[VISUAL: Neutral "status" graphic — a progress bar that stops partway, clearly unfinished.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Rana is held in Tihar Jail. In the first weeks, Mumbai Police and the NIA reportedly questioned him for eight to ten hours a day. Reports described his answers as evasive. Investigators took voice and handwriting samples, to match against intercepted communications with Headley. Solicitor General Tushar Mehta was appointed to lead the prosecution himself.

A supplementary chargesheet followed in July 2025. By October, India was still asking the US for more details. [PAUSE] As of that reporting, there is no verdict.

---

## CLOSING (13:45–14:45)

**[VISUAL: Return to the two silhouettes from the cold open, now with a line between them fading to grey.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
The trainers, the gunmen, the handlers in Karachi — everyone else in this story used violence directly. [PAUSE] Rana handed over something quieter. A business card. An office. A room at the Taj.

And behind all of it, a friendship from a boarding school in Punjab, still intact forty years later. An American jury decided that wasn't enough to make him part of the attack. [PAUSE] Now an Indian court has to decide: was the cover story a weapon?
