# RAW FILES — Episode 3 — Ravindra Kaushik ("Black Tiger") — Research Dossier

Prepared for The Sisodia Files. POV: pro-India; Indian sources first. Compiled 30 Sep 2026.
Method note: I read the Telegraph (29 Dec 2002), Hindustan Times (6 Dec 2009), The Pioneer (13 Aug 2012), Times of India (24 Jul 2012), India TV (27 Jul 2012) and ThePrint (11 Apr 2023) full texts. Others (Tribune, Open, Organiser, Patrika, Amar Ujala, Better India Hindi, Deccan Chronicle) were read through fetch summaries only. The web-search quota ran out before the last planned searches (Inayat Masih detail, Firstpost/News18/Swarajya). That gap is listed in section 8.
Deliberately OMITTED (per brief, no tradecraft): how he communicated, how he was equipped or trained beyond "language and religion", how he crossed.

**The single most important editorial finding:** the most detailed Indian account we have (ThePrint, Praveen Swami, Apr 2023, citing serving/former R&AW officers) says he was NOT a commissioned Pakistan Army officer. He held a *clerical* job in the Army's military accounts service. The "Major" claim comes from the family (2012) and from popular retellings. Script must not say "Major" as fact. Suggested line: "By the family's account, a Major. By R&AW's own officers, a clerk — in exactly the office that mattered."

## 1. Core facts (dated timeline)

Confidence key: [A] two or more independent Indian sources; [B] one Indian source (often family); [C] only derivative or repetition-chain sources.

- **1952, 11 April** — Born, Sri Ganganagar, Rajasthan. [A] TOI 24 Jul 2012 (nephew Vikram Vashishtha: "Born on April 11, 1952 in Sriganganagar") https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/jaipur/Salman-Khans-new-movie-in-controversy-again/articleshow/15114477.cms ; ThePrint "1952-born" https://theprint.in/opinion/the-cost-of-being-an-indian-spy-what-happened-to-ravindra-kaushik-raws-black-tiger/1511104/
- **Family** — Father J.M. Kaushik, ex-Indian Air Force, later employed at a local textile mill; family lived in the old city near the mill (Pioneer). Mother Amla Devi/Amladevi (d. 2006). Younger brother Rajeshwarnath (R.N.) Kaushik ("two years younger", HT), sister Shashi Vashishtha (mother of Vikram). [A] Pioneer https://web.archive.org/web/20120824211533/http://www.dailypioneer.com/nation/86994-late-spys-kin-fight-for-reel-life-credit.html ; HT https://www.hindustantimes.com/jaipur/the-real-life-behind-a-2002-spy-thriller/story-U9NVg1hsRRh4vizo9fRxhN.html ; TOI (sister Shashi).
- **1965–71 (teenage years)** — HT: grew up "between 1965 and 1971, when India went to war with Pakistan"; "became a fervent patriot" (HT's characterisation).
- **College** — B.Com at Seth G.L. Bihani S.D. Post Graduate College, Sri Ganganagar; known for mono-acting and mimicry. Classmate Sukhdev Singh: "He was one of the most popular students during his school and college days." [A] ThePrint; Pioneer; HT.
- **c.1973 — Spotted / recruited.** Two versions, both Indian:
  - Family version: a mono-act (nephew says at a national-level drama meet in Lucknow; TOI). Brother's version: "probably" a college mono-act where he played an Indian army officer refusing to give information to China (HT).
  - R&AW-officer version (ThePrint): he "came into contact with officials at the agency's Sri Ganganagar station and volunteered for service"; recruited 1973; two years of training.
  - Dates vary: 1971 (Pioneer, nephew), 1973 (ThePrint; Tribune 2026 via VK Singh's book), "joined RAW in 1975 as a 23-year-old" (Telegraph 2002). [Contested — see §8]
  - Training in Delhi for ~2 years incl. Urdu, religious education, Pakistan's geography (Pioneer; ThePrint).
- **1974/75 — Goes across.** ThePrint: cover identity as "Nabi Ahmad Shakir", Islamabad resident, equipped "early in 1975"; opening scene says he was sent across "that night in November 1975." Nephew says "around 1974" (Pioneer). Telegraph: alias "Nabi Ahmed". [A]
- **Karachi University, LL.B.** — Telegraph ("did his graduation in law"), Pioneer (nephew: "admission in Karachi University and completed his LLB"). [B/A] Note ThePrint says a "college graduate" who took a clerical Army job; it does not mention the LL.B.
- **Pakistan Army job** — ThePrint: R&AW officers say Kaushik wanted to apply for the officer-recruitment exams but R&AW "shot down" the idea because his papers "would not survive verification"; so he "applied for and secured a clerical job in the Pakistani Army", in the military accounts service. Pioneer (nephew): "commissioned officer… rose to become a Major." TOI: "recruited in defence services in Pakistan." Telegraph: "joined the Pakistan Army" (no rank). **Rank = contested.**
- **1976 (per Wikipedia only)** — married Amanat. Pioneer: "one Amanat, who belong to a well off family." ThePrint: "Amanat Nabi, the daughter of a soldier in his department." HT: "married a local girl." Child: one child — Wikipedia and Amar Ujala say a son; **Pioneer (nephew) says a girl**; ThePrint says "a child." [Contested]
- **Visits to India** — nephew: 3–4 visits, via Dubai on his Indian passport (Pioneer). [B — family only; not tradecraft, just a claim]
- **1979–1983 — Intelligence period.** Widely repeated (Wikipedia, Tribune, TOI, Pioneer). ThePrint's substance: from the military accounts office he could report "on the movements of military units, the postings of key officers and even the movements of trains with war material" — "a goldmine for the agency." [A on nature; the 1979 start date is the nephew's claim]
- **Sept 1983 — Exposure.** RAW operative Inayat Masih ("Inyat Masiha" in Telegraph), sent to contact Kaushik, is caught. Telegraph: Masiha "inadvertently blew the agent's cover." ThePrint: Masih's 1983 arrest was "a part of the story" of detection; R&AW's mid-1990s file review "do[es] not provide conclusive evidence for just how Pakistani counter-intelligence detected him"; some officials speculate his lack of relatives in the picture triggered surveillance. HT: Masiha "arranged to meet with Kaushik in a park", arrested. Kaushik was 29 (HT). [A]
- **1983–85 — Sialkot.** "Tortured for two years at an interrogation centre in Sialkot" (Telegraph). [A — Telegraph; Wikipedia copies it]
- **1985 — Death sentence**, later commuted to life. [A] Telegraph; ThePrint; TOI. (Nephew: appealed in Pakistan's Supreme Court; commuted "in 1990" — Pioneer; single source.)
- **1985–2001 — Prisons.** Telegraph: "dumped in Mianwali jail for another 16." Pioneer: Sialkot and Kot Lakhpat named. HT and TOI (nephew) say Multan jail. [Contested location]
- **1987 onward — Family petitions.** Telegraph: "all their pleas since 1987 to secure Kaushik's release… fell on deaf ears"; foreign-ministry replies said only that "his case has been taken up with Pakistan." [B]
- **2001, November — Death.** Pulmonary TB and heart disease, Central Jail Mianwali (Telegraph: "In November 2001… succumbed"). Exact date 21 Nov 2001 appears only in Wikipedia and Tribune (Mar 2026). [C for the day; A for month/year, but HT and TOI say 2002 — see §8]
- **Aftermath** — Father died (Telegraph: "of shock and heart failure"; HT: stroke two years after son's death). Family got a monthly allowance: Rs 500, later Rs 2,000, until mother's death in 2006 (HT). [A]
- **29 Dec 2002** — The Telegraph runs the first big Indian story, "India's forgotten spy" (Dalip Singh). Earliest source found. https://web.archive.org/web/2020/https://www.telegraphindia.com/india/india-s-forgotten-spy-agent-s-family-fights-an-impossible-battle/cid/849809

- **Jul–Aug 2012** — Nephew Vikram Vashishtha serves legal notice (22 Jul) on Yash Raj Films, CBFC etc. over "Ek Tha Tiger"; files writ in Rajasthan High Court; director Kabir Khan denies the film is his story.
- **11 Apr 2023** — ThePrint (Praveen Swami): "The cost of being an Indian spy." Project X context.

## 2. The defining contribution

What is solidly sourced (be careful — R&AW files on him "have not been declassified" per ThePrint):
1. **Project X's purpose.** ThePrint: Project X was "the super-secret R&AW effort to plant long-term resident agents at the heart of Pakistan's establishment", "designed to detect early signs of Pakistani military mobilisation" in the tense years after Zia's 1977 coup, with India fearing "he could go to war." Kaushik was the fabled face of it. It was shut down about 20 years before 2023 once aircraft and satellite sensors made its assets redundant.
2. **The output.** From the military accounts office, reporting on unit movements, key officer postings and war-materiel train movements — "a goldmine for the agency" (R&AW officer to ThePrint).
3. **The family's valuation (assertion, not proof).** Amladevi's letter: a spy "sitting in the right place… saves the life of at least 20,000 soldiers of his nation" (Telegraph). Present as her belief, attributed.
4. **What we can NOT source.** Kahuta nuclear details, "troop deployments in Punjab", "Operation Blue Star planning", "influenced Zia's policies" all appear in derivative 2026 pieces (Tribune, Open, Organiser, Patrika) with no primary source. The Patrika Blue Star/Zia claim is chronologically doubtful (Blue Star was June 1984, he was in custody from Sept 1983). **Do not voice them as fact.** Suggested framing: "India's own officers won't tell you which battle he changed. They say the material was gold. The file is still shut."
5. **The second, quieter contribution: the argument he forced.** His story, via a family that asked only for "recognition", became the template for the debate on how India treats caught agents (Karamat Rahi, Surjeet Singh, Roop Lal Sahariya, Kishori Lal Sharma, all in ThePrint). For a script hook: the paradox is that the spy who saw everything in Pakistan couldn't get one line of acknowledgment in India.

## 3. Cold-open candidates

**A. The last letter (strongest).** Three days before he died in a Pakistani jail, he wrote home: "Had I been an American, I would have been out of this jail in three days." Source: HT, 6 Dec 2009 (Presley Thomas). Pair it with the Telegraph's line that his earlier letter asked: "Kya Bharat jaise bade desh ke liye kurbani dene waalon ko yahi milta hai?" Visual: a folded letter, Hindi text, a brother in Jaipur reading it. (Caveat: HT places this in a Multan jail in 2002 — say only "a Pakistani jail".)

**B. The 1973 mono-act.** A student on a stage playing an Indian officer who refuses to talk under interrogation while unseen men in the audience take notes. Brother Rajeshwarnath: "It was probably his mono-act in college in which he played an Indian army officer who refused to divulge information to China that caught the attention of intelligence officers." (HT). Irony line: he rehearsed the interrogation years before he lived it. (Caveat: nephew says the show was in Lucknow; brother says in college; say "a stage".)

**C. The night before the border (ThePrint's opening).** A Tamil-speaking R&AW officer sits by a Border Security Force outpost, "protected by three armed guards", waiting for moonrise to shake hands with the agent he trained, "the man sent across the border that night in November 1975." (Swami, ThePrint.) It is Swami's reconstructed scene from officer accounts; label it "as R&AW officers describe it." Avoid detail on how he crossed.

**D. The pension.** After his death the government sent his parents Rs 500 a month, later Rs 2,000, "until 2006, when their mother Amladevi died" (HT). Rajeshwar: "But for the country, he was just another agent."

## 8. Contested points / caveats (India's account first)

1. **Rank.** India's family/popular account: commissioned, rose to Major (Military Accounts) — Pioneer/nephew, Tribune 2026 (citing M.K. Dhar's Open Secrets), Organiser, Open. **Counter (also Indian, and more authoritative on the agency's own view):** ThePrint/Swami, citing R&AW officers, says the officer route was blocked and he took a *clerical* job. Recommendation: say "Major, by the family's account" or avoid rank.
2. **When he joined RAW.** 1971 (Pioneer/nephew), 1973 (ThePrint, Tribune via V.K. Singh), 1975 (Telegraph). Recommend: "recruited in 1973, in the Sri Ganganagar area, went across in 1975."
3. **Place of death/jail.** Mianwali (Telegraph, ThePrint, Tribune, Wikipedia) vs Multan (HT 2009, TOI 2012 via nephew, Amar Ujala, Hindi Wikipedia "New Central Jail Multan"). Use Mianwali; note Multan in one line.
4. **Year of death.** 2001 (Telegraph, ThePrint, Wikipedia) vs 2002 (HT 2009: "2002 at the age of 50"; TOI/Pioneer nephew: "2002"). Telegraph's 29 Dec 2002 piece says "last year", which supports 2001. The date "21 November 2001" appears only in Wikipedia and Tribune 2026 — flag as single, derivative.
5. **"Black Tiger" and Indira Gandhi.** First appears in family statements in 2012 (TOI: awarded by RAW "and also by the then Prime Minister, Indira Gandhi"; Pioneer: code name "changed to Black Tiger" in 1979, no Indira). It is absent from Telegraph 2002, HT 2009 and ThePrint 2023. Wikipedia sources it to a dead CNBC-TV18 link. Indira Gandhi was not PM in 1979 (out from Mar 1977 to Jan 1980). Present as "reportedly."
6. **Pakistan's side (one line only).** Pakistan has never publicly acknowledged him in anything I found; Pakistani-side sources were not searched (quota). Do not claim Pakistan's position.
7. **Child.** Son (Wikipedia, Amar Ujala) vs daughter (Pioneer/nephew) vs unspecified (ThePrint). Avoid stating gender.
8. **Trial/commutation details.** "Closed military trial" and "commuted by Supreme Court of Pakistan" appear in Wikipedia/Deccan Chronicle derivative chains; Pioneer's nephew gives 1990 for commutation. Only the 1985 death sentence and its later commutation to life are solid.
9. **Kahuta / Blue Star / Zia claims** — unsourced in primary reporting. Do not voice.
10. **Ek Tha Tiger.** Family (nephew Vikram Vashishtha) says the film mirrors his uncle and asked for credit and a picture; Kabir Khan says the story is not his film and that he had never heard of Kaushik before. No court finding found. (Release date of Ek Tha Tiger, mid-Aug 2012, is from general knowledge, not fetched.)
11. **Dhurandhar (2025).** No official link. ## 9. Why the public barely knows the name

- **Government policy of silence.** Telegraph 2002: governments "rarely recognise in public secret agents… it is a common practice to disown spies caught in a foreign country." ThePrint: despite the family's lobbying of Vajpayee, "New Delhi refused to acknowledge he was a spy."
- **Files sealed.** ThePrint: "Files on the case have not been declassified."
- **He died unnoticed.** The first mainstream Indian story came a year later (Telegraph, Dec 2002), and the next big one in 2009 (HT), 2012 (film row).
- **No memorial.** ThePrint: "There's no memorial to the agents who gave their lives."
- **A small, poorly resourced family.** Rs 500/month; a brother in Jaipur; pleas since 1987 answered only with "case has been taken up with Pakistan."
- **Pop culture blur.** ThePrint: "For Indians schooled by reel life, the spy ends his story in Venice, Cape Town, Zurich, and London… The real-life ending is grimmer." Ek Tha Tiger (2012) and Dhurandhar (2025) fictional spies overshadow him; the name resurfaces only when a film releases.
- **Project X itself is closed** (shut about two decades before 2023), so no institutional memory or ceremony keeps his name alive.
