## 2. The defining contribution

Raman's mark is not one operation. He was R&AW's institutional memory on Pakistan-sponsored terror and its first public voice on it. Four strands.

**A. The Sikh-militancy / Pakistan file, and getting the world to believe it.** As desk chief and later CT head, he briefed foreign agencies on ISI backing for Khalistani groups. Some Western services were sceptical; Raman held the line that the data was double-checked (Amber Sen, The Hindu). In his 2003 congressional statement he set out the ISI's Punjab playbook: "Over half a dozen Sikh terrorist organisations came into existence. Their cadres were trained by the ISI in camps set up in Pakistani territory…" and the plot to hit civil aviation (five Indian Airlines hijackings to Lahore; Kanishka, 1985; the Narita plot). He also noted that the movement "petered out after 1995" as Indian and Western agencies cooperated. [House record, above]

**B. The external side of the 1993 Bombay blasts.** He drafted the PM's note the same evening and ran R&AW's external investigation. The 1994 Kathmandu-to-Delhi pick-up of Yakub Memon, which he says "was coordinated by me", produced the Memon-family evidence that helped tie the blasts to Pakistan and the ISI. India's account: an "intelligence coup" that PM Rao monitored personally (Rediff). Yakub "handed to Indian investigators a huge cache of documents, videos, photographs, passports" proving ISI's role (Times of India, 26 Jul 2015, citing sources).

**C. Becoming India's public intelligence commentator.** After 1994 he wrote a near-daily stream for SAAG, Rediff, Outlook online and Asia Times, sat on the NSAB and the 2000 intelligence-reform task force, and testified in Washington (2002, 2003). Stephen Cohen invited him to contribute a chapter to *The Future of Pakistan* (Amber Sen, The Hindu). He filled a gap: Indian officialdom did not explain itself, so he did.

**D. Breaking the memoir taboo.** *The Kaoboys of R&AW* (2007) was among the first insider books on R&AW. It states Pakistan's terror campaign "started in 1956 in Nagaland" (not 1989 in Kashmir), records the 1971 war as R&AW's "finest hour", and pushes for an oversight watchdog on IB and R&AW (via reviews).

Also worth a line: his integrity. Amber Sen and IPCS both stress an "impeccable" reputation for financial integrity, a "hard taskmaster and workaholic", and near-photographic recall ("he could tell you the contents of notes recorded by him many years ago").

## 3. Cold-open candidates

**A. The note on the night of 12 March 1993 (best, sourced, visceral).** 13:30, a car bomb in the basement of the Bombay Stock Exchange; by 15:40 twelve blasts across the city (Wikipedia). By that evening, in Delhi, one officer has drafted a detailed note for the Prime Minister: likely motives, a list of groups that could have done it, and similar attacks abroad. In the days after he "meticulously followed up on every small clue". VO hook: "By nightfall, he already knew what kind of enemy this was." [The Hindu, Amber Sen: https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/op-ed/remembering-a-perfect-spy/article4843663.ece]

**B. The man in the departure lounge, Kathmandu, July 1994.** Yakub Memon, on the run under ISI protection in Karachi, slips into Kathmandu to ask a relative and a lawyer whether the Memons can come home and surrender. They advise him to go back. Before he boards the Karachi flight, he is stopped. Weeks from retirement, the officer who "coordinated the entire operation" is Raman. Use only as published in his own article. [Rediff, Raman's own words: http://www.rediff.com/news/column/exclusive-b-ramans-unpublished-2007-article-why-yakub-memon-must-not-be-hanged/20150723.htm]

**C. The email at 9:39 am, 2 August 2007.** Sheela Bhatt's inbox: a bullet-pointed column arrives, followed by a note: "I wrote this, but I am having second thoughts. Don't use it. Others might escape as a result of this article…" It sits unpublished for 8 years. It surfaces days before the hanging. In it, the spymaster asks himself: "Would I be a moral coward if I did not do so?" [Rediff 2015]

**D. The tweets (emotional, works as a closing button too).** 12 Feb 2013: "I wonder whether when death ultimately comes to me, it will come from my cancer or from Chennai's mosquitoes." 24 Feb: "I am not yet prepared to walk into oblivion." 19 May: "Sinking. Slowly, but steadily. A losing battle." Hindu: he called the cancer "the last terrorist in his life". [Open, Siddhartha Gupta, 26 Jun 2013; The Hindu]

**E. (Optional pre-history) The mules.** Before the 1962 war, IB sources reported a "tremendous increase in the number of mules and Chinese muleteers" in Kachin State. They were "ridiculed and accused of nursing imaginary fears". They were actually Chinese Army and intelligence officers from Yunnan. Raman told this as the story of why India needed R&AW. [Kaoboys via Goodreads: https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/1932385-the-kao-boys-of-r-aw-down-memory-lane]

**Do NOT use:** the "R&AW selection test in a Calcutta lane" story. It is a fabricated illustration in the Winnowed blog review ("Okay, I made that one up").

## 5. People involved

- **R.N. Kao (1918–2002)** — First R&AW chief; Raman's mentor and the "Kaoboys" namesake. Per Raman's book, the name came from Ambassador Apa Pant, and stuck after CIA chief George H.W. Bush gave Kao a small bronze cowboy statue (via review, lrrp).
- **Indira Gandhi** — PM who created R&AW (1968); Raman admired her statesmanship.
- **Rajiv Gandhi** — PM 1984–89; Raman calls the Punjab/Pakistan chapter of his tenure the book's best (via review).
- **P.V. Narasimha Rao** — PM 1991–96; monitored the Yakub operation; praised R&AW's 1993 work.
- **Morarji Desai, V.P. Singh, I.K. Gujral** — PMs Raman criticises for weakening R&AW (via review).
- **Yakub Memon** — Chartered accountant, 1993 blasts conspirator; hanged 30 Jul 2015.
- **Tiger Memon; Dawood Ibrahim** — Masterminds; sheltered in Pakistan (Raman: Dawood "in Karachi", US Treasury designated him a global terrorist, 16 Oct 2003).
- **Sheela Bhatt** — Rediff journalist; Raman's 2007 interlocutor; published the article in 2015.
- **B.S. (Bahukutumbi Srinivasa) Raghavan** — Raman's elder brother, retired IAS; gave permission to publish.
- **Amber Sen** — Ex-Special Secretary, R&AW; wrote the Hindu tribute.
- **H.S. Bedi; Markandey Katju** — Former SC judges who backed leniency after the 2015 piece.
- **Vikram Sood** — R&AW Srinagar station chief credited (Wikipedia) with Siachen early warning; later R&AW chief. Rules out Raman for Meghdoot.
- **Stephen Cohen** — US scholar who commissioned Raman's chapter for *The Future of Pakistan*.
- **Jagjit Singh Chauhan** — Khalistan proponent that Raman says the Yahya regime and US officials backed (1970–77). Contested (§8).
- **A.Z. Phizo; Isaac Swu; Muivah** — Naga leaders in Raman's account of Chinese and Pakistani support.
- **Rep. James A. Leach** — Chaired the 29 Oct 2003 hearing where Raman testified.
- **Nitin Gokhale** — Author, *R.N. Kao: Gentleman Spymaster* (2019); the standard Kao biography.
- **Sachi Sri Kantha; Gen. Harkirat Singh** — Critics on Sri Lanka (§8).

## 8. Contested points / caveats (India's account first)

1. **Where Yakub was arrested.** India: the CBI says Yakub was arrested at New Delhi railway station on 5 Aug 1994; courts convicted (TADA court 27 Jul 2007), the SC upheld the death sentence (21 Mar 2013) and dismissed review and curative petitions (curative rejected 21 Jul 2015). Counter: Yakub said he surrendered in Nepal on 28 Jul. Raman's 2007 text says both are true in a sense ("informally picked up in Kathmandu", then "formally arrested in Old Delhi"). Note the brief's "Old Delhi" is Raman's wording; Wikipedia/CBI say New Delhi station.
2. **What Raman actually argued.** Not that Yakub was innocent. He wrote there was "not an iota of doubt" of guilt; his case was that Yakub's later cooperation, including persuading family to leave ISI protection, was a "strong mitigating circumstance" the prosecution did not put before the court. He also argued honouring a State's promise "strengthened its standing". He worried the piece could unravel the case ("Others might escape"). Do not present him as opposed to punishment for the 1993 blasts. Note his 2006 line on Afzal Guru ("kill them in action and not in custody…").
3. **Reaction.** Supporters: ex-SC judges Bedi and Katju; an unnamed ex-R&AW official told the Times of India "We have betrayed him". Counter: the State and courts held the sentence proper; the piece is an unpublished draft that Raman himself wavered on, released by a portal 8 years later with his brother's consent.
4. **ISI, Khalistan and the US.** India's account (Raman): Khalistan was a "joint covert operation by the US intelligence community and Pakistan's ISI" from 1971 (Kaoboys, via Indian Defence Review). Counter: Sikh organisations and many scholars dispute state framing, tying militancy to 1984 (Blue Star, Delhi riots); Pakistan denies ISI sponsorship. Use one line.
5. **Sri Lanka.** India's account: R&AW policy on Sri Lanka followed Indira Gandhi's security concerns (Trincomalee tanks, VOA, JVP) per Raman. Counter: Sri Lankan Tamil critic Sachi Sri Kantha (sangam.org, 3 Jul 2013) calls Raman "a nasty guy… a toady" and blames R&AW for damaging Tamil movements. Gen. Harkirat Singh's memoir is cited there. Raman's own operational role is undocumented. Hostile source; use only as "critics say".
6. **Meghdoot and Mumbai visit** are unverified (§0).
7. **Paris/Iran back-channel.** Wikipedia says "it is said" he was posted to Paris posing as *The Hindu* correspondent and built a back channel with "Ali Khamenei" and the Iranian revolutionaries using an Iranian student. No primary source; uses the wrong name for the Paris-era leader (Khomeini was at Neauphle-le-Château). Also The Hindu's own obituary does not mention it. Do not use without a book citation.
8. **CIA mole in R&AW Chennai, 1987; Major Rabinder Singh** are mentioned in Kaoboys per a review. Sensitive; skip or state as "Raman himself documents CIA penetration" without names.
9. **Bias caution.** Raman was a Nehru-Gandhi admirer (Indian Express) and a critic of some foreign-policy choices and of the BJP's "militaristic" policies (per one Pakistani reviewer, khalidmasood.com, a hostile reading). The channel should not present him as apolitical.
10. **Numbers.** 1993 blasts: official 257 dead, ~1,400 injured; Raman's 2003 column says "over 200". Use the official figure.
11. **Cancer type.** Open's subhead says prostate; his tweets and doctor say bladder with liver spread (first detected Oct 2009; recurrence Dec 2012).

## 9. Why the public barely knows the name

(Sourced facts first; inference marked.)

- **The trade forbids it.** Kao himself "shunned" publicity, avoiding interviews and photographs; his colleagues followed suit (Tribune review of Gokhale: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/book-reviews/the-kaoboy-who-founded-raw-871736 ; Raman on Kao: "never once did he drop or use their names").
- **Raman was reticent about himself.** A reviewer notes Kaoboys has "no information whatsoever about B. Raman the individual" and does not even explain what "B" stands for (Winnowed). Another finds Raman's own achievements "tucked in here and there in low key, often credited to his guru or his organisation" (lrrp review).
- **He kept his best material off the record.** Sheela Bhatt says he "insisted that I should use the data in my reports without attribution" and then withheld the Yakub piece for 8 years.
- **Unmarried, solitary, lived on a state-housing-board flat in K.K. Nagar and "most of the time spent on the computer"** (Mujtaba; Open). There was no family narrative for the media.
- **Attention-magnet was the wrong one.** The 2007 memoir was a specialist book; the biggest mainstream moment came only after his death (Yakub, July 2015). The Indian Express even led with "Key RAW man in '07", not his name.
- **Inference (label as such):** his medium (dense bullet-point columns, blog posts, Twitter) gave analysts a huge readership but no face. Kao has a biography, a memorial lecture and a planned biopic; Raman has neither. The R&AW's culture of anonymity and the Official Secrets Act constrain memoirs (a reviewer's remark, khalidmasood.com).
- **Counter-fact for the hook:** he was chosen by the US House of Representatives as India's witness on Pakistani terror (2003), and Indian Express called him "one of India's foremost security experts". Famous to the people who matter, a stranger to everyone else.
