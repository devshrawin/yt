# RAW FILES — Episode 10 (Finale): RABINDER SINGH — Research Dossier

Prepared for The Sisodia Files. POV: pro-India, Indian sources first. No operational tradecraft included (route/exfiltration is given only at the high level the Indian press itself states).

**Research-quality note (read first).** This case is thin on primary records. Almost everything rests on (a) Praveen Swami's contemporaneous Frontline report (Jul 2004), (b) Outlook / Rediff / Indian Express reports of 2004-06, (c) two books by ex-R&AW officers (R.K. Yadav, *Mission R&AW*, 2014; Amar Bhushan, *Escape to Nowhere*, 2012 — a novel), (d) anonymous-sourced Firstpost (2018) and ThePrint (2023) pieces. Several quotes below were extracted through an automated page summariser; **re-check every quote against the live page before putting it on screen.** I ran out of web-search budget before I could search Parliament records and Khanna quotes — those gaps are flagged. Sources that could not be opened directly (Firstpost, News18, Frontline) were read through Wayback copies.

Legend: **[C]** = confirmed (court record, official act, or multiple independent Indian sources agree). **[R]** = reported/claimed (anonymous sources, one book, one outlet). **[D]** = disputed/conflicting across sources.

## 1. CORE FACTS — dated timeline

**Background**
- Born Amritsar, affluent landed Jat Sikh family; father a retired Army lieutenant. [R] — Frontline 2004 ("affluent landed family of Jats"); Wikipedia. https://web.archive.org/web/20080329115754/http://www.hinduonnet.com/fline/fl2113/stories/20040702004403200.htm
- Army Major (Gorkha regiments per Wikipedia), later volunteered/went on deputation to R&AW. [R] Outlook says he "came to RAW on deputation in 1986", nearly 18 years in the agency; Rediff says permanently absorbed after deputation. https://www.outlookindia.com/national/the-raw-and-the-cooked-whose-spy-was-he-news-224186 ; https://www.rediff.com/news/2006/nov/01spy.htm
- **Operation Blue Star link — YES, but only in Frontline:** "He served with distinction in Amritsar during Operation Bluestar, the counter-terrorist assault on the Golden Temple in 1984." [R, single contemporaneous source; Frontline] Note internal tension: Frontline puts this in his post-Army/RAW phase, Outlook says RAW deputation began 1986 — so his exact status in 1984 (Army or RAW's Amritsar office) is **unresolved [D]**. Wikipedia's "Major in 1970 / Blue Star" line and the tfipost/GroundReport copies of it are derivative and contain an obvious error (rank "in 1970"). Do not assert he was "in" the assault itself; say "reported to have served in Amritsar during Blue Star".
- Postings reported: head of R&AW's Amritsar office; field operative West Asia / West Europe; First Secretary, Indian Embassy Damascus; later The Hague. [R] — Rediff 2006, Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabinder_Singh_(intelligence_officer)
- Rank at the end: Joint Secretary (South-East Asia desk), one of 15 Joint Secretaries in R&AW. [C for rank; the "15" from Indian Express via Sangam repost, [R]] https://sangam.org/defection-of-a-raw-agent-from-india/
- Wife Parminder Kaur ("Pammi"); lived first floor, Defence Colony, New Delhi. Wife reported to have worked with USAID; relatives (sister, brother) reported to be in US government/World Bank service. [R] — Outlook; Wikipedia.

**Suspicion and surveillance**
- 1992-93: daughter seriously injured in a road accident; Singh sought a Washington posting through then MoS External Affairs R.L. Bhatia; request refused. Bhatia on record to Frontline: "My interest in the whole affair was purely compassionate." [C — named on-record source, Frontline 2004]
- Early 1990s: alleged CIA "honey-trap" by a female case officer, Damascus or The Hague. [R — anonymous officials to Firstpost 2018; *unproven*]. Frontline (2004) offers a different theory: he was procuring US documents via a relative who worked at USAID, and RAW later suspected the flow ran the other way. [R]
- 2002: Outlook (via News18) reports he went on a US government-funded counter-hijacking course unrelated to his South-East Asia brief; an official says he had "no business" going. [R] https://www.news18.com/explainers/does-us-charge-in-pannun-case-show-double-standard-a-look-at-how-cia-hoodwinked-raws-rabinder-singh-8682739.html (Wayback copy read)
- Feb 2004 (Outlook, year implied): superiors notice unusual questions on matters outside his desk; heavy photocopying noticed by junior staff who reported it to counter-intelligence. [R] Frontline says the tip came from the photocopier use "attracted the attention of his subordinate staff".
- Dec 2003: RAW's Counter-Intelligence & Security division (CI&S) begins suspecting he is a CIA mole (Firstpost 2018 / tfipost). Jan 2004: office and Defence Colony house covertly wired. [R]. Surveillance length: "91 days… office, car, residence, gym" (B. Raman's review, Eurasia Review) vs "4-month surveillance" (Firstpost) [D — both roughly consistent with Jan-Apr 2004].
- Amar Bhushan (R&AW's No. 3, head of CI&S) led the investigation. [C] — ThePrint 2023; Eurasia Review (B. Raman).
- Apr 2004: Singh's request to visit the US for a "family function" (daughter's engagement per Yadav) is denied. [R] Outlook; ABC citing Yadav.
- **19 Apr 2004**: searched as he left the RAW building; personal files seized, returned next day. [R — Outlook]. Frontline notes Joint Secretaries and above were at that time not searched on exit at Lodhi Road; the rule was likely to change.
- No taped meeting with a handler was ever caught: "he was never caught on tape meeting his handler or making a delivery" (Firstpost anonymous source). Raman's review agrees no concrete proof of CIA contact was captured before he left. RAW's own assessment per Outlook: damage "isn't substantial"; Bhushan called what was passed "mostly minor". [R/C-ish — mutual agreement across Outlook, Firstpost, ThePrint]

**Disappearance** — DATES CONFLICT [D]
- **1 May 2004**: leaves Delhi in the early hours in a friend's car; crosses into Nepal at Nepalganj, per Firstpost 2018 (anonymous govt sources) and Yadav's book (ABC). [R]
- **5 May 2004**: fails to report to work after the long weekend (Outlook; Deccan Chronicle also says 5 May). [R]
- **14 May 2004**: "disappeared, the day after election results" (Indian Express 27 May 2004 report as reposted; Rediff 2006 also gives 14 May). [R] Recommend the script say: "In the first half of May 2004" and note that reports differ by days.
- **7 May 2004**: flies Kathmandu → Washington (Dulles) on Austrian Airlines flight 5032 (a connecting routing via Vienna is my inference, not stated in sources), using US passports issued 7 Apr 2004 in the names "Rajpal Prasad Sharma" and "Deepa Kumar Sharma". Reported landing at Dulles ~3:40 am on 8 May. [R — Firstpost; Yadav; Prabhu Chawla/New Indian Express; Millennium Post] The passports/visa/embarkation-card evidence was reportedly obtained by R&AW's Kathmandu station. ThePrint (Swami 2023) describes the Kathmandu-station finding as "confirmed by R&AW's Kathmandu station".
- Person named as US facilitator: CIA official David Vacala. [R] **Caution:** Firstpost says he now works for a private firm; he has been named by Indian press but not by any US authority. Say "reported to have been named" or avoid the name on screen.
- 27 May 2004: Indian Express first reports the disappearance; 6 Jun: Sunday Express reports his flight via Nepal. [R via Sangam repost]

## 2. THE DEFINING STORY

**Frame:** the man who was watched for four months, was never caught doing anything on tape, and still walked out of India — and India's answer was a novel.

Beats (each sourced above):
1. A joint secretary with a colourful past: Amritsar landowner's son, Army Major, field man in Damascus and The Hague, a "generous host" with famous parties, described by B. Raman as an "above-average field operative willing to take risks, but a mediocre desk analyst" (Rediff, 23 May 2005). Everyone in the agency liked him; nobody in the agency looked at what the photocopier was doing.
2. The photocopier. Junior staff report the boss copying files not on his desk and asking questions outside his brief. RAW's counter-intelligence wing starts a watch.
3. The watch that never got a proof. Cameras, taps, a monitoring post, dozens of watchers — yet no meeting with a handler on tape. Internally, the counter-intelligence chief wants to wait for the handler; other seniors want him arrested. Reported dithering at the top (Mishra's PMO reportedly sat on the request; Sahay's RAW "multi-headed").
4. 19 April: he is stopped at the exit and his briefcase searched. Files are returned next day. He now knows.
5. Early May: he is gone. Delhi Police and airports are not alerted for hours or days (Frontline). Days later RAW's Kathmandu station reconstructs the trip: US passports under borrowed names, a flight west on 7 May.
6. Fury, then paralysis. The RAW chief presses the CIA; the station chief feigns ignorance (Firstpost). The US denies. India dismisses him in June under Article 311(2)(c), the President's pleasure, no inquiry needed.
7. The long silence. Complaint filed 2006; Interpol refuses in 2007; proclaimed offender 2009. Narayanan's inquiry never published. In 2012 the man who ran the investigation — Amar Bhushan — writes it as fiction. In 2018 an anonymous official tells Firstpost he died in Maryland in 2016, forgotten by the agency that took him. Five years later ThePrint finds no paper trail for the crash.
8. Coda for the finale: the RAW that hunted a mole is the same RAW whose whole existence is defined by not being seen — the series ends on the man nobody can name.

**Series-finale hook:** Ten episodes about spies India celebrates. This one is about the one it would rather forget — and the only failure the agency has never been able to close.

## 8. CONTESTED POINTS / CAVEATS

1. **Disappearance date:** 1 May (left India; Firstpost/Yadav) vs 5 May (missed work; Outlook/Deccan Chronicle) vs 14 May (Indian Express/Rediff). Flight date 7 May is repeated everywhere but from the same Yadav/Chawla line. Script: "early May 2004".
2. **Dismissal year:** June 2004 (Raman, 5 Jun) vs 2005 (ABC, GroundReport). Use 2004; note 311(2)(c).
3. **Blue Star:** supported only by Frontline 2004 (contemporary, strong) and echoed by copies. Exact status in 1984 unclear.
4. **Honey-trap theory** is anonymous-source speculation ("possibly"/"perhaps"). Frontline's alternative (USAID relative) and Raman's "prized acquisition" are also unverified. There is no public evidence of how he was recruited.
5. **Quantity of documents:** 210 reports in 16 days (ABC/Yadav); 23,100 files on two laptops (Raman's review, Eurasia Review); "20,000+ files" (Firstpost); "212 files" (Satyaagrah — low reliability; also claims "a decade of undetected espionage", "networks collapsed", which Bhushan and RAW's own assessment contradict). RAW's own view via Outlook/Bhushan: damage "not substantial", "mostly minor". Do not claim he burned agents or networks.
6. **"57 officers"** (Yadav; repeated by Firstpost and News18) and "19 conspirators": Yadav's claim only. The Delhi HC dismissed the related petition as "hearsay". Present strictly as a claim.
7. **Surender Jeet Singh v. Ashcroft is probably NOT him.** Docket 03-71868 means the petition was filed in 2003, before the May 2004 exit; the petitioner says he was a Khalistan-watching informant for 13 years, "hid with friends for a year" and entered the US on his own passport — none fits a serving Joint Secretary who left in May 2004 on a US passport in another name. Wikipedia itself states "no official proof… that Surenderjeet Singh is an alias". Multiple Indian outlets (DNA, ABC, Substack) repeat the link uncritically. **Do not present it as fact; mention it, if at all, as an unproven claim — or drop it.**
8. **Death in 2016:** a single anonymous-source report (Firstpost 2018); ThePrint (2023) finds no accident record; India never issued a statement; no US record cited. Say "reported by Indian government sources to have died in a road accident in Maryland in late 2016; unverified."
9. **Named CIA officer (Vacala):** reported by Firstpost/Yadav/Chawla; a private person; US never confirmed. Prefer "the CIA's Kathmandu station chief, as named in Indian reports".
10. **Amar Bhushan's book** is labelled fiction. Facts vs fiction: Raman (Eurasia Review) treats the surveillance, the photocopying, the Kathmandu exit, the laptop analysis, and the RAW leadership disagreement as factual; names changed (Singh = "Ravi Mohan", Bhushan = "Jeevnathan/Jeev", wife "Vijita"); some methods deliberately withheld "for security reasons". The book is also, in Bhushan's telling, an attempt "to explain (not justify)" his own decisions (truescoopnews) — so it is a self-interested account. *Khufiya* (Netflix, 2023) is loosely based on the novel and departs further.
11. **Parliament:** nothing verified. **Delhi HC dismissal/pension ruling:** none found. State neither.
12. **Bhushan's role is self-serving** and Yadav's is adversarial (he was himself in dispute with RAW leadership and CBI; a CIA reviewer noted his book has no footnotes or bibliography — ABC). Balance both.
13. **R&AW as a "CIA vs India" story.** Indian press (Firstpost, News18 2023) links it to the Pannun case as a "double standard" angle. Fine as commentary, but don't imply the US admitted anything: the US "denied the allegations" (Firstpost).
14. Outlook's date stamp (5 Feb 2022) is a re-index; the underlying piece is 2004. ThePrint's dates as read by my extractor showed typos ("2014") — trust the article body, not my summary.

## 9. WHY THE PUBLIC BARELY KNOWS THE NAME

- No trial: he was never in an Indian court; the case died with the failed Interpol notice and a 2009 proclaimed-offender order.
- Narayanan's 2005 report was never published; no parliamentary white paper; R&AW has no public-accountability mechanism (R&AW is outside the RTI Act), and the Official Secrets Act amendments make retirees wary of speaking (ThePrint).
- The story broke in the news cycle of the 2004 change of government (NDA→UPA), was buried, and resurfaced mainly through ex-officers' books (Yadav 2014, Bhushan 2012).
- Embarrassment: a Joint Secretary, ex-Army, walked out despite 4 months of surveillance; the top brass reportedly dithered. Official memory is "the betrayal R&AW would rather forget."
- No photograph is in the public domain; no verified death record; no confession. The public knows him mainly as a Netflix character (*Khufiya*, Oct 2023).

## 12. VERDICT

**Is there enough verified material for 12-15 minutes on Rabinder Singh? Yes — if the episode is honest about what is unknown and built around the contemporaneous Indian record.** What is solid: rank and post; the surveillance and its outcome; departure from India in the first half of May 2004; dismissal under Article 311(2)(c) in June 2004; the 2006 complaint; Interpol's refusal (2007); the 2009 proclaimed-offender order; Narayanan's unpublished review; the reforms; the Bhushan and Yadav books; the *Khufiya* afterlife. What is thin: how he was recruited; what he actually passed; any Parliament record; the death; any licence-clean photograph of him; any court ruling on pension.

**Recommendation: Rabinder Singh as the finale, framed as "the file that never closed"**, with Khanna as a 60-90 second epilogue only if a bridge is wanted ("what the agency became"). Reasons:
1. Only Singh has a story with a beginning, middle, and unresolved end; a finale should be a mystery, not a CV.
2. Rich, named, on-record Indian journalism: Frontline (2004), Outlook, Rediff/Raman (2005), ThePrint (2023).
3. It fits a pro-India POV without inventing anything: India's counter-intelligence *did* catch him — the failure was of decision and follow-through, and the reforms followed. That is a mature finale for a ten-part series.
4. The uncertainty is itself the hook (dates, death, files, recruitment) and turns the sourcing weakness into narrative.
5. Khanna: no documented incident, no quotes, still serving.

**Hard rules for the script:** attribute every claim to its source; say "reported" for anything in §1 marked [R]; avoid the CIA officer's name or attribute it; do not link the Ninth Circuit case to Singh as fact; do not state a Parliament debate or a Delhi HC pension ruling; do not use `File:Rabinder Singh.jpg` as his portrait; state the 2016 death as unverified. **Before voicing:** open the Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha question archives (May 2004–2009) for a definitive Parliament line; re-verify the Frontline quotes from the original page; run the pronunciation audit (e.g. Nepalganj, Vacala, Parminder, Narayanan, Rupaidiha, Lodhi, Tharakan).
