# RAW FILES, Episode 9: A.S. DULAT (Amarjit Singh Dulat)
Research dossier for The Sisodia Files. Compiled 2026-09-30.

Method note. Search budget ran out at the 200-call session cap, so a few planned verifications (Hizbul ceasefire date, Vajpayee's Srinagar rally exact date, Lone assassination date) are marked VERIFY. The strongest primary text used is Dulat's own book "Kashmir: The Vajpayee Years" (HarperCollins India, 2015), full text on the Internet Archive, and "The Spy Chronicles" (HarperCollins India, 2018), also full text on the Internet Archive. Book quotes below were copied from that text (OCR spacing cleaned). Newspaper quotes that I could only see through a summariser are tagged [SECONDARY] and must be checked against the original before we put them on screen.

Series POV: Indian sources first; India's account first on contested points. No tradecraft included.

## 1. CORE FACTS (dated timeline)

- **December 1940.** Born in Sialkot (now Pakistan); father a judge, Justice Dulat; family left Rawalpindi in 1947 (flew Dakota to Lahore, then Delhi). Educated Bishop Cotton School, Shimla; Panjab University. Source: Outlook review of "A Life in the Shadows" (Naseer Ganai). https://www.outlookindia.com/national/book-review-spymaster-as-dulat-memoir-a-life-in-the-shadows-is-the-story-of-him-kashmir-and-doctor-sahab-weekender_story-255664 . Wikipedia gives only "1940": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Dulat
- **1965.** Joins the Indian Police Service, Rajasthan cadre (Wikipedia). Outlook says he qualified in 1964. Flag: one-year discrepancy, use "mid-1960s" on screen unless resolved. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Dulat
- **March 1969.** Joins the Intelligence Bureau (Wikipedia). His book credits M.K. Narayanan with sending him to Kashmir and being his roommate in his first two years in the Bureau. Source for the latter: Kashmir: The Vajpayee Years, Acknowledgements. https://archive.org/stream/KashmirTheVajpayeeYearsDulatA.S/Kashmir_%20The%20Vajpayee%20Years%20-%20Dulat,%20A.S__djvu.txt
- **1987.** First visits Kashmir on a familiarisation trip, in preparation for a posting as deputy director IB (his own words, Introduction, Kashmir: The Vajpayee Years).
- **May 1988.** Posted to Srinagar as head of the IB station (his own words: "posted to Srinagar in May 1988"). So the "IB Kashmir station chief, late 1980s" premise is CONFIRMED. Wikipedia and secondary sources say Joint Director, IB, Kashmir, 1988-1990. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Dulat
- **8 December 1989.** Rubaiya Sayeed, daughter of the newly appointed Union Home Minister Mufti Mohammad Sayeed, is kidnapped by JKLF militants in Srinagar. Dulat, then IB head in Srinagar, is part of the government's effort to persuade Chief Minister Farooq Abdullah to release jailed militants; Farooq initially refuses, then gives in on orders from Delhi (Dulat's account, KVY ch.3). Wikipedia article on the kidnapping: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_of_Rubaiya_Sayeed
- **Early 1990.** Dulat writes that his Srinagar tenure ended about two months after Jagmohan became governor and Farooq resigned (January 1990). He was back in counter-intelligence for a few months, then put in charge of the IB's Kashmir Group (the "K-Group") "from then on" (KVY, ch.4). In 1995 he was still heading the K-Group when the al-Faran kidnapping of six Western trekkers happened (KVY, ch.3).
- **1990s.** In the K-Group he becomes the government's quiet interlocutor with separatists; Shabir Shah is his first big contact ("entered Kashmiri politics in 1990 through Shabir Shah", Wikipedia). On Prime Minister Narasimha Rao's instructions he later offered Shah a route into politics, which Shah rejected (Scroll review says Dulat tried to persuade Shah to contest the 1996 assembly election). https://scroll.in/article/1040973/former-ib-and-raw-officer-as-dulats-memoir-reveals-his-abiding-passion-for-troubled-kashmir
- **Rose to IB Special Director** (Wikipedia). Book confirms he was "number two in the IB" before RAW.
- **July 1999.** Kargil war ends in Indian victory. Dulat: "the army won the Kargil war in July, and in August I took over as chief of R&AW." (KVY, ch.2). He succeeded Arvind Dave, who was Secretary (R&AW) 1997-1999 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Research_and_Analysis_Wing). NOTE: the "1999-2000" tenure is therefore about 16 months. Also note IB-to-R&AW moves were unusual; he says R&AW staff feared he would "bring his own gang from the IB" and he did not.
- **August 1999, first days at R&AW.** Uses the main lobby lift rather than the chief's private lift; makes his staff officer Jayadeva Ranade, the supposed ringleader of the R&AW Service faction; visits founder R.N. Kao (then nearing ninety) in Vasant Vihar, who tells him: "You've got the best job in the government." (KVY ch.2).
- **12 October 1999.** General Pervez Musharraf takes power in Pakistan in a coup. Dulat, "two months" into the job, admits R&AW "was caught unawares" (KVY ch.2). This is a rare candid admission and useful for the episode.
- **24 December 1999.** IC-814 (Kathmandu to Delhi) hijacked at about 5 p.m. Lands at Amritsar, stays on the ground 45 to 50 minutes (Dulat's figure); The Wire's timeline (drawing on K.P.S. Gill and the 2003 NDTV interview of Brajesh Mishra) gives touchdown 7:01 pm, take-off 7:49 pm, i.e. 48 minutes. Dulat sits on the Crisis Management Group (CMG) headed by Cabinet Secretary Prabhat Kumar. https://m.thewire.in/article/security/ic-814-indian-airlines-hijacking-security-failure
- **25-29 December 1999.** Lahore, then Dubai (27 passengers released; body of Rupin Katyal, killed by hijackers, offloaded), then Kandahar. Negotiating team in Kandahar includes Ajit Doval, Nehchal Sandhu, C.D. Sahay of R&AW, Vivek Katju of MEA and civil aviation officials (KVY ch.3).
- **~28-29 December.** Doval presses Dulat by phone from Kandahar: "Take a decision quickly, sir, because these fellows are getting impatient and I don't know what will happen." (KVY ch.3).
- **30 December 1999.** NSA Brajesh Mishra asks Dulat to go to Jammu to persuade Chief Minister Farooq Abdullah; Foreign Minister Jaswant Singh phones Farooq on behalf of the CCS. Dulat's R&AW aircraft lands in Jammu at sunset in Ramzan. The Tribune of 31 Dec 1999 reported "a sudden visit" by the R&AW chief to Jammu on 30 December. https://www.tribuneindia.com/1999/99dec31/j&k.htm
- **30 December, ~10 p.m.** Farooq, after three-plus hours of shouting, goes to Governor G.C. "Gary" Saxena to resign; Saxena, a former R&AW chief, calms him with a drink and the argument that Delhi must have no other option. Farooq relents (KVY ch.3).
- **31 December 1999, morning.** Masood Azhar (from Kot Bhalwal jail, Jammu) and Mushtaq Zargar (from Srinagar) are put aboard Dulat's R&AW Gulfstream, blindfolded, seated behind a curtain; Dulat sits in front. In Delhi they transfer to Jaswant Singh's aircraft, where Omar Saeed Sheikh (from Tihar) is waiting. Jaswant Singh flies to Kandahar; hostages freed that day (KVY ch.3).
- **New Year's Eve 1999.** Dulat: "After a week of drama and intense pressure, I got home in time to bring in the New Year with my wife, sipping cognac by the fireside." (KVY ch.3).
- **2000, summer.** Hashim Qureshi and Hizbul Mujahideen operational chief Abdul Majid Dar return to India/the fold; Dar later announces a ceasefire (KVY ch.4, ch.7). Exact date of the Hizbul ceasefire announcement: VERIFY (I recall 24 July 2000, unconfirmed here).
- **13 December 2000.** Vikram Sood takes over as RAW secretary (Wikipedia table), so Dulat's RAW term ended in the first half of December 2000. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Research_and_Analysis_Wing
- **November 2000.** Vajpayee's unilateral Ramzan ceasefire in Kashmir, which lasted about six months, to May 2001 (KVY ch.15).
- **January 2001 (VERIFY) to May 2004.** Dulat becomes adviser on Kashmir in the PMO under Vajpayee. Sources conflict: Wikipedia says "January 2000 to May 2004", Scroll says January 2001 to May 2004, and Dulat's own book has him "by that time in the Prime Minister's Office" in "the winter of 2001". Since Sood took charge in Dec 2000, January 2001 is the logical date; treat Wikipedia's "2000" as an error until confirmed. https://scroll.in/article/1040973/former-ib-and-raw-officer-as-dulats-memoir-reveals-his-abiding-passion-for-troubled-kashmir
- **14-16 July 2001.** Agra summit between Vajpayee and Musharraf; ends without agreement (Spy Chronicles endnote). Dulat says the PMO's disappointment was shared by Vajpayee and Brajesh Mishra.
- **2002.** Dulat's stated brief in the run-up to the 2002 J&K assembly election: "obtaining broad-based participation" (KVY ch.15). In 2002 Abdul Ghani Lone was assassinated at a Srinagar memorial rally (Dulat describes the scene, KVY ch.8; exact date VERIFY, believed 21 May 2002).
- **April 2003.** Vajpayee's Srinagar rally at Sonawar stadium; Dulat is specifically asked to go with him; the PM speaks without a written speech and extends "the hand of friendship" to Pakistan (KVY ch.11).
- **May 2004.** UPA takes power; Dulat's PMO stint ends.
- **3-4 July 2015.** "Kashmir: The Vajpayee Years" (with Aditya Sinha, HarperCollins India) excerpts cause a row. Dulat says the government "goofed up" at Amritsar; BJP's M.J. Akbar and Yashwant Sinha reject the criticism; Congress demands a PM apology. ## 2. THE DEFINING CONTRIBUTION

**Thesis for the episode: Dulat is the spy whose weapon was the telephone. India's real Kashmir intelligence craft in the 1990s and 2000 was contact, and in the week that mattered most he used that contact to close a deal India did not want to make.**

Specifics:

1. **The Kashmir "K-Group" model.** In 1990, when the Valley was "in anarchy", Dulat was reassigned to head the IB's Kashmir Group. He says it became "the most active group in the IB, drawing in the best talent, advising prime ministers throughout the 1990s on initiatives in J&K" (KVY ch.4). The approach, sometimes called the Dulat model, was engagement over "maar do goli salon ko". Tribune review: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/book-reviews/spymaster-as-dulat-says-it-from-heart-in-his-memoir-a-life-in-the-shadows-468471
2. **Talking to militants who then came in.** As RAW chief he writes that talking to Kashmiris "had helped create a conducive atmosphere" for two returns in 2000: Hashim Qureshi and Abdul Majid Dar of the Hizbul Mujahideen (KVY ch.4). Dar's return led to the Hizbul ceasefire announcement, which fed the atmosphere for Vajpayee's unilateral Ramzan ceasefire and for the 2002 election (KVY ch.15: "Many things helped create the atmosphere for the 2002 election, including Majid Dar's coming back from Pakistan and announcing a ceasefire, Vajpayee's unilateral Ramzan cease-fire in November 2000, and the 2001 Agra summit."). Keep any operational detail out; the book itself says details are withheld under the Official Secrets Act.
3. **The hijack: the one job nobody wanted.** After the plane left Amritsar, India's options collapsed; Dulat's account is that once it left, "the only way it could have ended was with India giving into the terrorists' demands". The Kandahar negotiators, led on the ground by Doval, whittled the demand list from 35 prisoners plus 200 million dollars down to three men: Masood Azhar, Omar Saeed Sheikh and Mushtaq Zargar. Dulat's own special contribution was on 30 December in Jammu: he personally carried the Government of India's request to the one man with legal authority over Zargar and Azhar (both in J&K custody), Chief Minister Farooq Abdullah, and got it through after three hours of shouting and a midnight walk to Raj Bhavan. He says: "I was picked to do the dirty job as I was reckoned to be close to Doctor Saheb since my days as the IB head in Srinagar in the late 1980s." He flew the two prisoners to Delhi himself.
4. **The parallel with Rubaiya, ten years earlier.** Dulat writes the symmetry himself: "In both episodes he was chief minister... In both episodes I had asked him to release jailed terrorists, and in both episodes he had initially refused, saying presciently that doing so would have dire consequences for militancy in his state. In both episodes I persuaded him to ultimately give in, on orders from the top in Delhi." That gives the episode an arc, and a moral weight (he has said he found Zargar "highly distasteful", KVY ch.3).
5. **The PMO years, 2001-04.** He was Vajpayee's Kashmir adviser and became the channel to Hurriyat leaders (Mirwaiz Umar Farooq, Abdul Ghani Bhat, the Lone family, Shabir Shah, Yasin Malik) and to Farooq. Vajpayee's formula "insaniyat ke dairey" (within the ambit of humanity) at Srinagar airport and the April 2003 speech are the visible end of that work (KVY ch.11). Jan 2004 photograph of Vajpayee meeting Hurriyat leaders in Delhi is on Commons (see Photos).
6. **Historian's caveat.** The payoff of this policy is contested (see section 8); the channel produced ceasefires and participation in the 2002 election but not a settlement.

## 3. COLD-OPEN CANDIDATES

**A. Sunset, 30 December 1999, Jammu (best).** Ramzan. A small R&AW aircraft lands as the sun goes down. The chief of India's external intelligence walks into the Chief Minister's residence and finds Farooq Abdullah sitting alone at the dining table. "I know why you've come," Farooq says. "Just let me go and say my prayers." Then, after the prayers and a glass of juice: "You again? Tumne Mufti ki beti ke liye kiya tha, phir wohi kar rahe ho." Three hours of shouting; at 10 p.m. the two men drive to Raj Bhavan, where the governor, himself a former R&AW chief, pours Black Label. Source: Dulat, Kashmir: The Vajpayee Years, ch.3 (archive.org text). Cross-check: Tribune 31 Dec 1999 report of the visit, Scroll review of "The Chief Minister and the Spy".

**B. 24 December 1999, 7:01 pm to 7:49 pm, Amritsar, the 48 minutes.** Inside the Crisis Management Group room in Delhi the debate goes on while a plane sits on a runway 400 km away. Dulat's own verdict: "On 24 December 1999... the CMG debated how to deal with the hijacking, and while the CMG was debating, IC-814 flew away." Punjab Police chief Sarabjit Singh, who had walked out of a dentist's appointment to take the news, waits for orders from Delhi that never say "do not let it leave". Dulat: "That was the only moment when India could have taken control." Sources: KVY ch.3; The Wire timeline (7:01 pm landing, 7:49 pm departure).

**C. 31 December 1999, morning, blindfolds and a curtain.** Two prisoners are led aboard a small Gulfstream. "Azhar and Zargar were not allowed to see me. They were blindfolded and put on board before I got on the aircraft. There was a curtain in the cabin, and they were made to sit in the back. I sat in the front." Hours later he is home with cognac by the fire. Source: KVY ch.3. (Good as an emotional close to the cold open or as the mid-point turn.)

**D. August 1999, the lift.** New RAW chief refuses the private lift, walks through the main lobby "to make a point". Comic, human, sets his character before the crisis. Then, months later, the hijack. Source: KVY ch.2.

## 5. PEOPLE INVOLVED

- **Atal Bihari Vajpayee.** Prime Minister 1998-2004, Dulat's boss at the PMO; his "insaniyat" Kashmir line, Ramzan ceasefire (Nov 2000) and Agra summit (July 2001) frame Dulat's PMO work.
- **Brajesh Mishra.** Vajpayee's Principal Secretary and India's first National Security Adviser; told Dulat to go to Jammu on 30 Dec 1999 and coined the "poplars" line; ran the PMO-level Pakistan and Kashmir file.
- **Jaswant Singh.** External Affairs Minister; phoned Farooq for the Cabinet Committee on Security, personally escorted Azhar, Zargar and Sheikh to Kandahar on 31 Dec 1999; later defended the decision.
- **L.K. Advani.** Home Minister and Deputy PM; per Dulat opposed the release and chaired the crisis after the plane left Amritsar ("To give credit to the home minister, L.K. Advani, he landed up at the CMG and took charge after the plane left Amritsar").
- **Farooq Abdullah.** J&K Chief Minister (1996-2002); Dulat's friend of about 40 years ("Doctor Sahab"); refused, raged, then relented.
- **G.C. "Gary" Saxena.** J&K Governor (1998-2003), himself a former R&AW chief; calmed Farooq on the night of 30 Dec 1999.
- **Ajit Doval.** IB officer on the Kandahar negotiating team in 1999, later NSA; Dulat's colleague and later his open critic on Kashmir policy ("muscular" line).
- **Nehchal Sandhu, C.D. Sahay, Vivek Katju, Anand Arni.** Members of the Kandahar team (Sandhu and Sahay named in KVY; Arni reported as R&AW's man at Kandahar by Tribune/Deccan Herald reporting on the 2024 series).
- **Prabhat Kumar.** Cabinet Secretary; headed the Crisis Management Group.
- **Sarabjit Singh.** Punjab DGP in Dec 1999, Dulat's batchmate; waited for instructions from Delhi during the 48 minutes at Amritsar.
- **Parkash Singh Badal.** Punjab Chief Minister; told the DGP to "be careful", no harm to passengers.
- **Shyamal Datta.** Director IB; asked why the tyres were not punctured.
- **Nikhil Kumar.** NSG Director General; NSG commandos were not in position at Amritsar (KVY names him as one target of blame; DailyO writer names "Neeraj Kumar", a different name; use KVY).
- **Capt. Devi Sharan.** Pilot of IC-814; praised by Dulat: "his role was exemplary".
- **Rupin Katyal.** Passenger killed by the hijackers.
- **Masood Azhar, Ahmed Omar Saeed Sheikh, Mushtaq Ahmed Zargar.** The three freed prisoners; Dulat calls Zargar "a particularly brutish, ruthless, low-life Kashmiri terrorist".
- **Sharad Yadav.** Minister who flew to Dubai to receive the released hostages.
- **R.N. Kao.** RAW's founder, visited by Dulat in Aug 1999.
- **Arvind Dave, Vikram Sood.** Dulat's predecessor and successor as RAW Secretary.
- **Jayadeva Ranade.** Dulat's staff officer at RAW; China expert.
- **Mufti Mohammad Sayeed and Rubaiya Sayeed.** Home Minister and daughter in the 1989 kidnapping; Dulat's first meeting with Mufti was in early 1990. Dulat leaves the Muftis out of his inner circle.
- **Shabir Shah, Abdul Ghani Lone, Mirwaiz Umar Farooq, Abdul Ghani Bhat, Yasin Malik, Firdous Syed (Babar Badr), Hashim Qureshi, Abdul Majid Dar, Sajad Lone.** Separatists and militants on Dulat's contact list.
- **Asad Durrani.** Former ISI Director General (1990-91), co-author of The Spy Chronicles; barred from leaving Pakistan and later penalised.
- **Aditya Sinha.** Journalist; collaborator on both books.
- **Pervez Musharraf, Nawaz Sharif.** Pakistan's Army chief who seized power on 12 Oct 1999, and the PM he overthrew.
- **Zahid Hussain.** Pakistani journalist in Kandahar in 1999 whose book Frontline Pakistan Dulat cites for ISI control of the hijack.
- **Manmohan Singh.** PM after 2004; called Dulat "just before the 2002 (assembly) elections" (Spy Chronicles); released The Spy Chronicles in 2018.
- **Yashwant Sinha.** Became External Affairs Minister after Jaswant Singh moved to Finance; Brajesh Mishra told Dulat to brief him on Kashmir (Spy Chronicles).
- **Rahul Gandhi.** Walked with Dulat during the Bharat Jodo Yatra (Free Press Journal headline); date VERIFY. https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/india/who-is-as-dulat-aka-mr-kashmir-all-you-need-to-know-about-former-raw-chief-spotted-with-raga-in-bjy

## 8. CONTESTED POINTS AND CAVEATS (India's account first)

1. **Amritsar: whose failure?** India's government/BJP account in 2015: MJ Akbar said "consultations were held at the highest level" and asked whether "around 200 Indians" should have been left to die; Yashwant Sinha said "there was absolutely no other option, except to brittle down their demand" (India TV and Tribune, July 2015, both SECONDARY summaries). Dulat's account: the CMG "debated" and never gave Punjab a clear order; he told India Today in 2024 that he agreed with then-DGP Sarabjit Singh that he "was awaiting orders from Delhi, which never materialized" [SECONDARY]. Counter-view: former Punjab police chief K.P.S. Gill argued Punjab police should have acted (cited in The Wire, and in Dulat's own book). For the film: give the government's rationale first (no casualties was the stated priority, per Sarabjit Singh via KVY), then Dulat's verdict.
2. **Was there a warning?** Dulat and Anand Arni deny R&AW's Kathmandu station gave a hijack warning; the Netflix series says it did (Wikipedia, ThePrint, Deccan Herald reporting). The Wire notes a 27 September 1999 letter warning of a possible Kathmandu hijack within 2-3 months; nobody seems to have acted on it. So: intelligence warnings were general, not specific, and the record is contested. Treat all three as unresolved.
3. **Advani's role.** Dulat claims Advani opposed the release and stayed away when it was decided (Spy Chronicles, 2018). I found no on-record Advani reply in the sources I could open (an HT 22 July 2015 piece titled 'Advani didn't want to release terrorists for IC-814 hostages' returned 403). Present strictly as Dulat's claim, and note that Dulat's own book credits Advani with taking charge of the CMG after the plane left Amritsar.
4. **Author-of-Kashmir-narrative disputes.** Dulat's 2015 claims that intelligence agencies pay Hurriyat leaders and mainstream Kashmiri parties (Express Tribune, DailyO) and that Syed Salahuddin once asked IB for a medical seat for his son (HT) were denied by Hizbul and Hurriyat; the moderate Hurriyat rejected his claims about political integration (India TV). Present as allegations he made, not proven facts.
5. **Spy Chronicles.** Indian critics (Hindu Post: "Pakistani Deep State smoke and mirrors propaganda"; Catch News: "hard to take ... seriously"; opinion pieces) argue that the book gives an ex-ISI chief a platform and reads as Pakistani deep-state messaging; the book's most explosive line is Durrani's suggestion that Pakistan may have helped the US find bin Laden (Wikipedia summary). Dulat's line is that the book's purpose is "looking at things positively". India denied Durrani a visa; Pakistan punished him. Ex-PM Nawaz Sharif and PPP's Raza Rabbani criticised Durrani for co-authoring with an Indian (Scroll, 26 May 2018). https://hindupost.in/politics/pakistan-murders-civilians-border-secular-cabal-gathers-delhi-launch-durrani-dulat-book/ ; https://www.catchnews.com/news-point/pov/it-is-hard-to-take-dulat-and-durrani-s-chronicles-seriously-here-s-why-115293.html (Catch News page timed out on fetch; headline only, verify text). The Hindu Post page returned 403 on fetch, so only its headline is used and quoted.
6. **Politics and posture after 2014.** Dulat openly favours talks with Pakistan and separatists and criticises Doval's "muscular" approach (memoir chapter on Doval, Scroll, Tribune and Wikipedia summaries; The Wire full text unfetchable). In May 2025 he said he had always been "ideologically" inclined toward Congress (Organiser, hostile source). In June 2025 he praised Asim Munir's meeting with Trump (Tribune, from Geo News). That stance is likely to be unpopular with much of our audience; the episode should keep his record (August 1999 to 2004 service) separate from his current commentary and let viewers decide, without straw-manning either.
7. **The "first spymaster memoir" claim.** Dulat says "No spymaster has ever written a memoir. I have broken this tradition" (Tribune, March 2023); Outlook says he is "the first Indian spymaster to write a memoir". Other senior former officers have written memoirs (for example B. Raman's book on R&AW, and earlier officers' books), so say "first RAW chief to write a memoir" only after checking, or attribute it to the reviewers.
8. **Numbers.** Hostages: Dulat says 176 passengers held (KVY ch.3); Scroll's review says 189; Daily Excelsior says 155 plus crew. Use "about 176" and attribute; do not use 189 without a source note.
9. **Date discrepancies.** PMO start (2000 vs 2001), IPS year (1964 vs 1965), the 2015 India Today "goofed up" interview date. All flagged above.
10. **DailyO's "what Dulat left out" (14 July 2015, R.K. Yadav)** alleges an R&AW officer aboard the plane suppressed an advance warning, that Dulat was appointed RAW chief "at Farooq Abdullah's behest despite opposition", and that Pakistani diplomat Mohammad Arshad Cheema orchestrated the hijack. These are one commentator's claims, not established facts; do not use without independent confirmation. https://www.dailyo.in/politics/ic-814-hijacking-raw-as-dulat-nepal-kathmandu-kandahar-hijack-terrorism-hafiz-saeed-atal-bihari-vajpayee-lal-krishna-advani-bjp-pakistan-farooq-abdullah-4987
11. **Ajit Doval "would regret" Kandahar.** Wikipedia's article says Dulat recalled Doval "would regret" how the negotiations went. I could not find the primary text of that line, so do not use it. What is verified is the "Take a decision quickly, sir" quote in the book.
12. **Operational secrecy.** Dulat himself says he cannot disclose details of CMG proceedings and much of his RAW-era work under the Official Secrets Act. So the dossier is a story of contacts and decisions, not methods, and the script should stay that way.

## 9. WHY THE PUBLIC BARELY KNOWS THE NAME

1. **Short RAW tenure.** August 1999 to about December 2000, roughly 16 months, and the biggest event in it, IC-814, was a failure he did not own alone. Compared with Kao, Sankaran Nair or B. Raman, there is no "RAW built" story attached to him.
2. **IB man in RAW clothes.** He spent 30 years in the Intelligence Bureau, so nobody thinks of him as "RAW". His fame is inside Kashmir, not in Delhi drawing rooms, where he is "Mr Kashmir" (Free Press Journal headline).
3. **His best work is invisible by design.** Telephone contacts, tea with Hurriyat men, a ceasefire that arrives by news agency: none of this makes a film. Kandahar makes a film, but the hijack film (Netflix 2024) cast him as a fictional "V K Agarwal".
4. **Late memoirs.** First book only in 2015, at about 75, and it lands as a "goof-up" headline rather than as the story of the man.
5. **Polarising after 2014.** His pro-dialogue line, criticism of Doval and Pakistan-friendly commentary have made him a partisan name; mainstream nationalist narrative does not celebrate him, while some commentators treat him as an unreliable dove.
6. **RAW anonymity.** Chiefs' names are rarely public until they retire; the agency's own myth is built around Kao.
7. **Overshadowed by Doval.** The man who pleaded for a quick decision from Kandahar became NSA; Dulat, whose phone call to Farooq made the release possible, did not.
