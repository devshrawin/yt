# THE ACCIDENTAL CHIEF: N.F. Suntook and the Years RAW Almost Ended
### RAW Files · Episode 5 — long-form documentary script

**Runtime target:** 8–10 minutes
**Tone:** Investigative, sober. Pro-India point of view; Indian sources first (B. Raman's writing for ORF, DailyO, Yatish Yadav's history of R&AW, Shashank Mattoo's research on the Mauritius mission). Facts from `research.md`. Suntook left no memoir and gave no interviews; where the record is thin, the script says so. Through-line: the officer who held a hated agency together until its friends came back.

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00–1:30)

**[VISUAL: A newspaper front page; a headline card; Parliament House; an empty chair.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
March 1983. A newspaper in Calcutta runs a front-page story. India's spy chief, it says, has been working for the CIA. [PAUSE] He has fled the country, through Kathmandu, with a suitcase of top-secret files.

Other papers follow. In Parliament, the Home Minister has to stand up and answer. The man, he says, is abroad. On a government assignment.

The story is false. According to B. Raman, a former R&AW colleague, it was planted by a disgruntled subordinate. The man at its centre is in the middle of a secret mission on Indira Gandhi's own request. Days later he returns, and retires on schedule, farewell party and all. [PAUSE]

His name was N.F. Suntook. He ran R&AW for six years. And almost no one knows what he did. [PAUSE] So how does the head of India's spy agency vanish from history? Everything here comes from B. Raman's own account, Indian journalism, and the few historians who have looked. It starts six years earlier, with a wrecked agency.

## SEGMENT 1 — THE ACCIDENTAL CHIEF (1:30–3:00)

**[VISUAL: Dossier portrait — N.F. Suntook (silhouette; no photograph in the public record). The Indian Frontier Administrative Service.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Naushervan Framji Suntook was a Parsi. He was not a policeman. That alone made him unusual. Most of R&AW's leaders came from the Indian Police Service. Suntook came from the Indian Frontier Administrative Service, a cadre created to run the country's north-east. According to some accounts, he had also served in the Navy.

DailyO would later call him "an accidental chief." The description is fair. He was not the founder. He was not the visionary. [PAUSE] He was the man who was in the room when everything fell apart.

There is no biography of Suntook. No interview. No known photograph. [PAUSE] What survives is the story of six years.

**[LOWER THIRD: "N.F. Suntook · Indian Frontier Administrative Service · RAW chief, 1977–1983"]**

## SEGMENT 2 — THE HOUSE THAT DESAI INHERITED (3:00–4:45)

**[VISUAL: Morarji Desai; Indira Gandhi; a checklist — 1977: the Emergency ends · the founder goes · the deputy goes · the chief's rank is cut.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
In March 1977, the Emergency ended. Indira Gandhi lost the election. Morarji Desai became Prime Minister. And Desai despised R&AW. Before the election, he had called it Indira Gandhi's "praetorian guard."

He did not abolish it. According to B. Raman, he cut it. The budget was trimmed. The staff was reduced. Some operations in neighbouring countries were stopped. [PAUSE] And the leaders went.

Kao was pushed out in April. His deputy, K. Sankaran Nair, took over. Nair lasted about three months. He resigned rather than preside over the agency's downgrade. In his place, the government appointed Suntook, at a lower rank than Kao had held. DailyO would later call him a "pliable officer." [PAUSE] It was not a compliment.

**[CITATION CARD: Sources — B. Raman, "On the RAW Hunt" (ORF, 2004); DailyO, 2016]**

## SEGMENT 3 — THREE YEARS OF HOLDING ON (4:45–6:30)

**[VISUAL: Charan Singh; Indira Gandhi in 1980; the RAW emblem; a calendar, 1977–1980.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
For the next three years, Suntook did the least glamorous job in intelligence. He kept the building standing.

Yatish Yadav's history of R&AW puts it in one line. Desai, Yadav writes, put Suntook in charge. And somehow, Suntook "saved R&AW from being plundered by its own customer, the government." [PAUSE] The customer was the Prime Minister.

According to B. Raman, the agency's files were reviewed by the new government's ministers. Home Minister Charan Singh, after going through the record, is reported to have told Kao that the allegations against R&AW had been wrong. [PAUSE] It was a small vindication. It did not restore the budget.

The Janata government fell in 1979. In January 1980, Indira Gandhi returned to power. Kao came back as her security adviser. Now, R&AW's friends were in office again.

**[LOWER THIRD: "1977–1980 · R&AW survives the Janata years"]**

## SEGMENT 4 — THE MISSION (6:30–8:15)

**[VISUAL: Mauritius; a farewell party card, postponed; Anerood Jugnauth; Indira Gandhi.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
By 1983, Suntook was due to retire. The date was March 31. A farewell party had been arranged. [PAUSE] The evening before, Indira Gandhi called him in.

According to Raman, she asked for one last task, a sensitive mission to a friendly country. His service was extended by a few days. The party was postponed.

The country was Mauritius. It was in political turmoil. Prime Minister Anerood Jugnauth, a friend of India, faced a leftist rival whom Delhi distrusted. In Shashank Mattoo's account, Suntook worked with India's High Commissioner in Port Louis to help build a broad coalition of Indian-descent leaders around Jugnauth. [PAUSE] Delhi had also drawn up contingency plans for a military response, Operation Lal Dora.

It did not come to that. In August 1983 Jugnauth won a decisive election, and Mauritius stayed close to India.

**[CITATION CARD: Sources — B. Raman; Shashank Mattoo; Brewster and Rai, "Operation Lal Dora"]**

---

## SEGMENT 5 — THE RUMOUR (8:15–9:15)

**[VISUAL: Newspaper front pages; a clarification in Parliament; the farewell party, held.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Then the rumour hit. While Suntook was out of the country, a story spread that he had defected. The press did not retract it, even after the government raised the matter with the Press Council.

On April 7 he flew home. On April 8, according to Raman, he retired, and the farewell party went ahead. [PAUSE] The nation's spy chief had been called a traitor on the day he was serving his country.

He retired to Gurgaon. His wife died in 1991. Raman writes that by 1993 he was ill, and alone. A newspaper ran a story about a forgotten spymaster. [PAUSE] By 2004, Raman was calling him "the late N.F. Suntook."

---

## CLOSING (9:15–10:15)

**[VISUAL: The empty chair from the cold open; the silhouette; the R&AW emblem.]**

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
Kao built R&AW. Nair walked out for principle. B. Raman explained it to the world. And Suntook did the only thing left to do. [PAUSE] He stayed.

No memoir. No interview. No photograph. Almost nothing except a story planted by an enemy, and a farewell party postponed until the work was done.

Some agencies are remembered for what they achieved. [PAUSE] Was this one saved by a man history forgot?
